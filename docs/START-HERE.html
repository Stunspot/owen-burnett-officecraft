Start here: Owen Burnett Officecraft v0.1.0

Owen Burnett Officecraft turns ordinary office requests into a disciplined production job: a document, deck, workbook, PDF, or a coordinated packet whose consequential facts still agree. You supply the subject truth and approval; Owen organizes the work, chooses the right artifact route, and records what actually happened.

Use Officecraft when the request sounds like any of these:

  • "Turn these notes into a decision memo."
  • "I need a slide deck for Monday."
  • "Make this spreadsheet readable and auditable."
  • "I need a polished PDF and the editable source."
  • "Make these four office files say the same thing."

Officecraft v0.1.0 is file-production focused. It does not replace your calendar, inbox, HR, accounting, database, or live collaboration systems. It never publishes, emails, uploads, shares, overwrites an external artifact, or marks a deliverable approved without your separate authority.

What to give Owen

Give the material you have: notes, pasted text, existing files, CSV data, PDFs, images, template or brand guidance, and the requested result. Include the audience, deadline, and whether an editable source is required when you know them. You do not need to pre-sort the mess.

Owen infers routine presentation choices. He asks a focused question only when the answer changes the factual meaning, authoritative source, artifact family, template authority, privacy boundary, compatibility requirement, or an irreversible external action.

The one sentence that matters

Ask plainly. For example:

I need a two-page leadership brief and a six-slide decision deck from these notes. The audience is the operations leadership team. Keep the workbook figures authoritative, use this template, and do not send anything outside this workspace.

The result should identify the editable source, any derivative such as a PDF, checks actually run, remaining gaps, and the approval still yours to make.

Readiness boundary

This guide describes the product and its intended workflow. It is not proof that a particular host installed the skills, created a file, opened native Office, connected a cloud account, or delivered anything externally. Read verification and readiness before making a stronger claim.
